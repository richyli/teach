Content Product Planning and Content Strategy (2026) · 2026

1. Course Description

This course (Content Product Planning and Content Strategy), taught in English, walks students through a complete product-planning lifecycle: taking a content-product idea from market research through MRD, PRD, wireframe, scope, and risk to a roadmap. Its defining feature is that Agentic AI (Claude Code) is used as a working partner throughout — each week pairs a lecture with a flip exercise or role play so students learn, by doing, when to lean on AI and when human judgement must take over. The course also brings in 22 theoretical lenses from communication, information systems, and STS (including critical frames such as inscription, surveillance capitalism, algorithmic imaginaries, postphenomenology, and algorithms of oppression), so students not only build products but also reflect on the values, biases, and political economies baked into AI-mediated content products. As a capstone, each team designs a "Judge Skill" — a Claude Code skill that evaluates other teams' proposals against the rubric — turning the idea that "to design is to inscribe values" into practice.

Note: Because Agentic AI (Claude Code) is used in nearly every session, students are strongly advised to subscribe to a paid Claude plan for the duration of the course. The free tier's usage limits are unlikely to sustain the weekly hands-on workload.

2. Course Objectives & Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Theoretical literacy — Apply 22 theories to analyse AI-driven content products and choose the right lens for the right design question.
  2. End-to-end product planning — Take an idea from market research → MRD → PRD → wireframe → scope → risk → roadmap with Agentic AI as a partner at each stage.
  3. AI skill craftsmanship — Design, share, and critique Claude Code skills for PM tasks, culminating in a Judge Skill that evaluates other teams' pitches.
  4. Calibrated trust — Decide when to lean on AI (drafting, synthesis, persona generation, red-teaming) and when human judgement must override it (real-user validation, ethical decisions, scope negotiation under conflict).
  5. Critical / ethical reflexivity — Use STS frameworks to surface the values, biases, and political economies in AI-mediated content products.
  6. Defend under fire — Argue product decisions against peer Q&A, red-team challenges, and role-played scope negotiation.
  7. Interpersonal & negotiation skills — Communicate product decisions clearly to cross-functional stakeholders; negotiate scope and priorities under competing interests (PM vs boss vs engineering lead); set and hold boundaries — saying "no" upward, protecting an MVP from scope creep, and defining where human judgement must override AI.

4. Assessment Tools & Grading Criteria

Component Weight Type / When
Midterm Pitch (MRD) 15% Group, W8
Final Pitch + Judge Skill 30% Group, W16
Theory Reports × 2 20% Pair, rotating W3–W15
AI Skill Sharing × 2 20% Individual, two slots across the semester
Class Participation & Flip Exercises 15% Individual, weekly

Each component is scored on 1–4 dimension rubrics (4 Excellent, 3 Proficient, 2 Developing, 1 Needs work); full rubrics are in the Rubric / Grading section below. Assessment strategy: group midterm/final pitches thread the full lifecycle together, while Theory Reports and AI Skill Sharings distribute the load and train oral defense and tool craftsmanship; in-class red-team and role-play activities ensure non-presenting students are graded on contribution. AI use is expected, not penalised — what is penalised is opaque, unreflective AI use.
