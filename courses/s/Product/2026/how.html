How to learn in this course

Everything for this course lives on this site: the readings, the slides you would otherwise be handed, and the exercises are all the same pages. Do these seven things and the semester gets much easier.

1. Bookmark this site

One address for the whole semester. The weekly schedule, the units for each week, the exercises and the assignments all start here.

On a phone, add it to your home screen; on a laptop, put it in the bookmarks bar. Do not plan to find it by searching — these pages are not necessarily indexed.

2. Bring a laptop or tablet; this page is the handout

There is no separate printed handout and no slide deck emailed afterwards. In class, open that week's unit and scroll — that is the running order.

A phone works, but the units contain tables and interactive tools that are painful on a small screen. Bring a laptop or tablet if you can.

3. Use AI to study, and use the prompts the course gives you

Every unit has a pre-class AI section with a prompt you can copy whole into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Those prompts are designed: some of them make the AI fail on purpose, so that you can catch the failure in class.

Using AI is expected here. What is graded is whether you can say what you asked, where it was wrong, and why you kept or overrode its answer. Pasting output you cannot audit is what costs marks.

4. Read ahead — especially the vocabulary

Each unit opens with learning objectives and a vocabulary block. Five minutes on those two before class is enough.

The terms link to glossary cards: one term per page, English above and 中文 below. Not following a class is usually not about difficulty; it is about a word nobody defined for you.

5. Finish the interactive tools during class

Some units contain a quiz, a simulator or a drag-and-match. They are not decoration — they are that section's practice, so do them there and then, not afterwards.

The one you get wrong is worth more than the ones you get right: it tells you which assumption you were carrying.

6. Join the discussion at the end of each unit

Each unit closes with a pair discussion and an individual reflection. Every discussion starts from a given situation, so you never have to invent your own material — just work through it.

When you pair up, say your judgement out loud rather than nodding along. The part you cannot put into words is the part you have not understood yet.

7. Keep your own record of what you learned

Every unit ends with one sentence worth taking away. Keep them in one place — a notebook, a notes app, Notion, whatever you already use.

Read them again at the end of the semester and you will find you have overturned some of them. That overturning is what you actually learned this term.
