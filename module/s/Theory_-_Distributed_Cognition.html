Distributed Cognition

A short primer on Distributed Cognition: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Edwin Hutchins (1995) — "Cognition is not in the head — it's spread across people, tools, and representations."

The core idea

The unit of analysis is the activity system, not the individual mind. Cognition happens as representations propagate and transform across people, artefacts, and media.

  • A cockpit remembers its speeds — not because any pilot does, but because the system does
  • Knowledge lives in checklists, dashboards, dialogues, gestures
  • Move a tool, lose a memory

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Primary theory in W16 Final Presentation — a rotating pair opens those sessions with a 15-minute report on it.
  • Supporting theory in W1 Course Intro & PM Role — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.

The full week-by-week list is in Theory Report Schedule.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. Where does this knowledge actually live — in heads, hands, files, screens, or rituals? 2. Which representations propagate through the system, and how do they transform at each handoff? 3. What breaks if I remove this artefact — and was that breakage planned? 4. Is the AI a member of the cognitive system or a black box bolted on the side?

Recommended reading

  • Hutchins 1995, Cognition in the Wild — the founding monograph (the ship-navigation case)
  • Hollan, Hutchins & Kirsh 2000, ACM TOCHI 7(2) — distributed cognition as a foundation for HCI (see Hollan 2000 Distributed)
  • Hutchins 1995, "How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds," Cognitive Science 19(3)

(Hutchins scholar note: Edwin Hutchins)

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. System-level design — design AI features as members of a cognitive system; specify what propagates between human and agent 2. Representation map — for each step, name the representation and its medium; ask where AI transforms it 3. W16 Judge Skill as system — recognise the evaluation harness itself as a distributed cognitive system, with its own breakage modes

⚠️ Failure mode: treating "AI augmentation" as a property of one user, instead of a redesign of the whole system.

Discussion

  1. In your project team, where does institutional memory actually sit? What happens if Claude rotates out?
  2. If cognition is distributed, what is a "user" in user research?
