Affordance Theory

A short primer on Affordance Theory: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

James J. Gibson (1979); Ian Hutchby (2001); Paul Leonardi (2011) — "Tools afford actions — what does this AI invite you to do?"

The core idea

An affordance is a relation between a user's capabilities and an artefact's properties — not a feature of the artefact alone.

  • Gibson: affordances are perceived possibilities for action in the environment
  • Hutchby's "tech turn": technologies have affordances that constrain and enable
  • Leonardi: distinguish perceived vs actualised affordances

A button affords pressing only for an actor who can press.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. What does this artefact afford — for which user, in which situation? 2. What's the gap between perceived and actualised affordance for our target user? 3. What does it also afford that we did not intend? 4. Which constraints are we counting on the material to enforce?

Recommended reading

  • Hutchby 2001, Sociology 35(2) — affordances as the bridge between social construction and technological realism
  • Bucher & Helmond 2017, in SAGE Handbook of Social Media (see Bucher Helmond 2017 Affordances)

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Affordance teardown — for any AI tool you adopt, list its perceived, hidden, and refused affordances before deciding scope 2. Wireframe-by-affordance — design screens by writing the verb list first, then the UI 3. Misuse audit — what does this Skill afford that violates your policy? Document and constrain materially, not just by guidelines

⚠️ Failure mode: collapsing affordance into "feature." The whole point is the relation.

Discussion

  1. Pick an AI product. What does it afford to a novice that it does not afford to an expert?
  2. If affordances are relational, can a roadmap promise an affordance — or only the conditions for one?
