Affordance Three-Party Role Play

This unit covers a two-round negotiation between a platform, a creator, and an audience representative over what one AI feature actually affords, and what happens to that agreement when the ranking weights change underneath it.

🎯 Learning objectives

  • What you will be able to do: tell a feature apart from a perceived, hidden, and actionable affordance, and explain how one function affords completely different things to different actors.
  • The real problem it lets you explain: why a platform can say "we only changed the ranking" while creators experience it as having their livelihood cut off — how a change in algorithmic weighting alters what a product affords without a single pixel changing, and why the people affected cannot articulate what was done to them.

Core

  • The three layers of affordance, using the same vocabulary as Content Products and Affordance Teardown: perceived, hidden, actionable.
  • Leonardi's distinction between perceived and actualised affordance (see Affordance Theory): when three parties perceive the same function differently, the behaviour each actualises differs too.
  • Boundary object: the single sentence the three parties have to negotiate in Round 1 is an object each reads through their own lens while still sharing it.
  • Algorithmic opacity: a change that can be felt but not pointed at is the most common form of power on a content platform.

Theory

Primary: Affordance Theory (Gibson, Hutchby, Leonardi) — this week's Primary Theory. Supporting: Boundary Object Theory, Surveillance Capitalism (the gap between a platform's internal metrics and its public account of itself).

🤖 Pre-class AI workout

Paste each of these into a chatbot before class and bring the answers, plus your own objections, as raw material.

  1. Pick an app you open every day. List five things it lets you do that are NOT in its feature list. Then argue, as that company's PM, that you never designed any of them.
  2. You are the product lead of a reading platform. I am a writer whose reach just dropped 40%. Defend a ranking change that hurt me — without admitting it hurt me, and without lying.
  3. Give me three ways a platform can change what a feature affords without changing a single pixel of the interface. For each, say how a user could detect it, and how they could not.
  4. Explain Leonardi's perceived vs actualised affordance using a product I name. Then tell me where the distinction breaks down.

Role play — Platform × Creator × Audience

Vera Reads, a fictional cross-market reading platform, has launched an AI recommendation feature (the For You shelf). Work in threes. Each of you holds a private card with four fields: Goal, Your reading of the feature, Communication norm, Do not reveal.

  • Platform Product Lead — you need a sentence that can go into release notes without committing to disclose how ranking works.
  • Creator — you need a sentence you can hold them to later.
  • Audience Representative — you need a plain-language sentence you can read aloud to a readers' committee.

The third field on the card is where the friction comes from. It is a company norm, not a national culture — for example, "in this company nobody objects in the room; dissent is submitted in writing within 24 hours." Play the company, not a country.

The two rounds

Round 1 — each party states what this feature affords, and the three of you negotiate one public description that all three will sign.

Injection — the Platform Lead alone receives a weighting-change card: completion rate weighted up, recency weighted down, zero change to the interface. The other two each receive a signal card describing something that feels off but cannot be named.

Round 2 — renegotiate the same sentence. The Platform Lead may not raise the change unprompted, but may not deny it if asked directly.

Reveal — the contents of the injection cards are read out.

✍️ Take-home task

Hand in one sheet per group before you leave: (1) the shared affordance sentence from Round 1; (2) the revised sentence from Round 2; (3) one sentence from each of the three of you naming which thing the others said that changed your position.

After class, submit the Affordance Teardown three-layer map and the redesign proposal for a non-obvious audience, as set out in Content Products and Affordance Teardown.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Swap your two sentences with another group.

  1. Did their second sentence get vaguer or sharper?
  2. If it got vaguer, who made it vaguer, and what did that person gain?
  3. Point at the line that most reads as written to avoid disclosing something

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: "I only realised today that a product I use may already have changed something I cannot see, and my only clue would be __." Then a second: "The hardest thing to say out loud today was the communication norm on my card, specifically ____."
