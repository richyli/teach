Stakeholder Management and Actor Networks

This unit covers the power/interest grid and RACI, why datasets and regulators count as stakeholders, and how to manage upward and sideways when some of the actors are not people.

Core

  • Power/interest grid; RACI.
  • Treating AI, datasets, platforms, and regulators as non-human stakeholders.
  • Managing upward and sideways.
  • Primary theory: Actor-Network Theory (Latour, Callon) — stakeholders include non-human actors, and ANT supplies the vocabulary for mapping them. Supporting: Service-Dominant Logic (relationships as value co-creation rather than zero-sum), Boundary Object Theory.

Hands-on — Actant Mapping

  1. Draw an ANT diagram of your own product
  2. Include at least three non-human actants: datasets, APIs, model providers, regulators
  3. Other teams challenge you in public: what actant did you miss?

✍️ Take-home task

Submit the ANT diagram plus the actants you added after being challenged.

Case

Opus 4.7 Claude Code Hackathon — "domain knowledge is the moat". MaestrIA's diagnostic rules came from interviewing the founder's father, a mechanic; ARIA encoded a senior engineer's intuition. What had a moat was not the code the AI wrote but the domain knowledge that came from people.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Swap actant maps with the team next to you.

  1. Find a non-human actant they treated as a neutral tool that can in fact govern them — an API version change, a model provider raising prices, a regulator whose single sentence takes a feature down
  2. Circle the actant whose power they most underestimated
  3. Say which quadrant of the power/interest grid it really belongs in

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: "I used to treat __ as background, and I now see it as an actant that can decide whether this succeeds; the next thing I will do to manage it is ____."
