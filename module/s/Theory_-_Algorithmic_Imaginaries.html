Algorithmic Imaginaries

A short primer on Algorithmic Imaginaries: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Taina Bucher (2017, 2018) — "What users imagine the algorithm does shapes their behaviour — even when they're wrong."

The core idea

Users do not act on what algorithms actually compute. They act on folk theories — imaginaries — about how the algorithm works. These imaginaries are productive: they have real effects on posting, clicking, and consuming behaviour, regardless of accuracy.

  • The algorithm is felt, sensed, narrated — not read as code
  • Users adapt their behaviour to fit their imagined algorithm (the "if-then" they construct)
  • Imaginaries circulate socially: friends, journalists, creators, "growth hackers"
  • Accuracy is not the point — productivity is

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Supporting theory in W6 Understanding Audiences & Users — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.
  • It is never the week's Primary theory, so no report is assigned to it. It still earns marks when it does real work in a pitch.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. What do my users imagine this algorithm or AI feature does? 2. Where does the imaginary diverge from the reality — and which gap is dangerous? 3. Am I designing for the user's actual workflow, or for the workflow they think the algorithm rewards? 4. What myths are circulating in my user community right now, and where did they come from?

  • Bucher 2018, If…Then: Algorithmic Power and Politics — book-length treatment
  • Schulz 2023, "A New Algorithmic Imaginary" New Media & Society — extension to generative-AI era

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Surface user imaginaries via interview — ask "how do you think Claude decides what to say?" before asking about features 2. Design affordances that align imaginary with reality — or at least do not actively deceive; transparency UI is imaginary-management 3. Treat creator/power-user folk theories as a leading indicator — when "everyone says" the algorithm rewards X, X is about to flood your data

⚠️ Pitfall: building features around what users should imagine the algorithm does, while ignoring what they actually imagine — your beautiful mental model never reaches them.

Discussion

  1. Pick an AI-feature your users interact with daily. What do they imagine it does — and how did you find out (or guess)?
  2. If imaginaries are productive regardless of accuracy, is "explainability" really about explanation, or about installing the right imaginary?
