Scope Management and Prioritisation

This unit covers scope creep and the MVP, two prioritisation frameworks, saying no upward, and what happens to all of it once Agentic AI makes building cheaper.

Core

  • Scope creep; MVP; prioritisation with RICE and MoSCoW.
  • The affordable-loss principle; saying no upward.
  • How Agentic AI changes the cost of building.
  • Primary theory: Effectuation (Sarasvathy) — under AI uncertainty, scope is set by the means at hand and by affordable loss, not by pre-fixed goals. Supporting: Activity Theory, Boundary Object Theory.

Role play — PM × Boss × Eng-Lead Tug-of-War

Work in threes. Each of you holds a private card: the PM holds a user-pain card, the Boss holds a CEO-deadline card, the Eng-Lead holds a tech-debt card.

  1. Negotiate a final scope
  2. Record what each side gave up and why
  3. The class votes on the smartest compromise and the worst escalation

✍️ Take-home task

Submit the final scope decision plus each side's reason for conceding.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Swap final scope decisions with another team.

  1. Ask about one feature they cut: can this version really ship without it?
  2. Score their trade-offs with RICE or MoSCoW
  3. Name one item that should be pulled back into the MVP, or one that should still be cut

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: "Next time I face the boss's deadline card, I will say no upward by ______." Then a second: the thing you noticed today about where you concede too easily, or refuse to concede at all.
