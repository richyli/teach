Synthetic Data and AI Personas

This unit covers generating synthetic users with an LLM, the four validity threats that make them untrustworthy, and where the ethical line sits when a generated respondent stands in for a real person.

Core

  • Generating synthetic users with an LLM; AI-persona focus groups and simulated interviews.
  • Statistical synthetic data: augmentation, filling in rare segments.
  • Validity threats: mode collapse, bias amplification, sycophancy, pseudo-consensus.
  • Ethical guardrails when a synthetic respondent replaces a real one.
  • Primary theory: Para-social Interaction (Horton & Wohl) — synthetic users exploit our tendency to treat AI characters as social actors. Supporting: Sociomateriality, Algorithms of Oppression (Noble, on whose voice is amplified and whose is erased when the persona pool comes from a biased model).

Hands-on — Synthetic Focus Group versus Real Interview

  1. Run a ten-person AI-persona focus group with an Agentic AI
  2. Interview two real people on the same questions, extending the Mom Test from W6
  3. Report the two side by side: what the synthetic group caught, what it missed, and what it invented
  4. Say which decisions could safely rest on synthetic data and which could not

✍️ Take-home task

Submit the side-by-side synthetic versus real report.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Swap side-by-side reports with another team.

  1. Find the insight in their synthetic group that is too clean and too unanimous — the likely pseudo-consensus or sycophancy
  2. Find the one a real interview contradicted
  3. Name one point where the synthetic group was confident and the real person disproved it

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: "If I had to hand in real user research next week, I would use synthetic data at __ and I would hold the line on real people at ____."
