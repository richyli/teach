Theory Report Session

This unit covers how the weekly theory report runs, what the reporting pair owes the room, and how everyone else earns marks while someone else is presenting.

A rotating pair opens every session from W3 to W15

The pair or trio on duty gives a 15-minute student-led report on that week's Primary Theory: the original source, the core mechanism, one classic empirical study, one criticism, and two discussion prompts that tie the theory to that week's product topic.

The full week-by-week list, the supporting theories, and a primer page for every theory are in Theory Report Schedule.

The session runs in six stages so that listening is graded too

Pre-class question submission → the 15-minute talk → an AI translator round → a red-team challenge → a theory-in-use map → a one-line synthesis. If you are not presenting, stages 3 to 6 are where your participation mark comes from.

How the time is spent

Stage What happens
The report The pair on duty presents source, mechanism, one classic study, one criticism, and two discussion prompts
Interaction rounds AI translator round, then red-team challenge, then the theory-in-use map; non-presenting students score on contribution
Pair review Restate the mechanism to each other in one sentence, then name the strongest and the weakest point in the report
Synthesis The reporting pair closes with one sentence: can this theory explain this week's product case, and where does it break?

Grading

The report feeds the "Using Theory in Product Sharing" rubric — two reports at 10% each, 20% of the course grade. Dimensions and what a 4 looks like are in Theory Report Schedule and in the syllabus.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Find a partner as soon as the report ends.

  1. Restate the theory's mechanism to each other in one sentence
  2. Name the most convincing point in the report, and the least defensible one
  3. Check the reporting pair's two discussion prompts — do they actually connect to this week's product topic?

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: "The one mechanism from this week's theory most likely to change how I look at my own product is __, because ____."
