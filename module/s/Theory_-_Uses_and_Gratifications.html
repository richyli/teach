Uses and Gratifications

A short primer on Uses and Gratifications Theory: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Katz, Blumler & Gurevitch (1973–74) — "What gratifications does this content actually serve?"

The core idea

Media use is goal-directed. Audiences actively select content to satisfy felt needs — they are not passive vessels.

Five canonical need clusters: - Cognitive — information, understanding - Affective — emotion, aesthetic experience - Personal-integrative — identity, status, self-confidence - Social-integrative — connection, belonging - Tension-release — escape, diversion

The flip from "what media do to people" → "what people do with media."

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Primary theory in W6 Understanding Audiences & Users — a rotating pair opens those sessions with a 15-minute report on it.

The full week-by-week list is in Theory Report Schedule.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. Which of the five gratifications does this feature plausibly serve? 2. For whom, in which moment of the day, in which mood? 3. Are we substituting one gratification for another (e.g., social → escape)? 4. What gratification gap are competitors leaving open?

Recommended reading

  • Katz, Blumler & Gurevitch 1973–74, Public Opinion Quarterly 37(4), "Uses and Gratifications Research" — the canonical statement
  • Ruggiero 2000, Mass Communication & Society 3(1), "Uses and Gratifications Theory in the 21st Century" — internet-era reboot
  • Sundar & Limperos 2013, Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media 57(4), "Uses and Grats 2.0" — new affordance-driven gratifications

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Gratification enumeration — ask the AI to list every gratification a feature could serve, then prioritise; do this before writing a feature spec 2. Persona stress-test — generate AI personas with explicit gratification profiles and walk them through the journey 3. Re-label the metric — for each KPI, name which gratification it is actually measuring

⚠️ Failure mode: confusing observed engagement with served needs. High DAU may mean habitual tension-release, not satisfied cognition.

Discussion

  1. Pick an AI chat product. Which of the five gratifications dominates? Which is the platform pretending to serve but isn't?
  2. If gratifications are self-reported, can we trust an AI persona to report its own?
