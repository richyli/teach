Surveillance Capitalism

A short primer on Surveillance Capitalism: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Shoshana Zuboff (2015, 2019) — "Behavioural surplus → prediction products → markets in human futures."

The core idea

Surveillance capitalism is a market logic that renders human experience as raw data, extracts a behavioural surplus beyond what is needed to serve the user, and sells prediction products in markets for future behaviour.

  • Rendition — turning lived experience (clicks, voice, gait, gaze) into machine-readable data
  • Behavioural surplus — the extracted data beyond service-improvement use
  • Prediction imperative — competition drives ever-deeper extraction and ever-more-certain forecasts
  • Instrumentarian power (vs totalitarian) — shapes behaviour without belief
  • Big Other — the ubiquitous sensate computational architecture

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Supporting theory in W3 Content Product — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.
  • It is never the week's Primary theory, so no report is assigned to it. It still earns marks when it does real work in a pitch.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. What behavioural surplus does this feature extract beyond what the user is paying for? 2. Are we selling a service to the user, or predictions about the user to someone else? 3. What does the prediction imperative push us to collect next — and have we said no anywhere? 4. Where does our system modify behaviour rather than merely serve a request?

Recommended reading

  • Zuboff 2015, "Big Other" Journal of Information Technology 30(1) — see Zuboff 2015 Big Other
  • Zuboff 2019, The Age of Surveillance Capitalism — the full theoretical statement
  • Zuboff 2019, New Labor Forum shorter essay — accessible summary; see also Zuboff 2019 Surveillance Challenge

(Zuboff scholar note: Zuboff, Shoshana)

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Audit your AI feature's extraction — list every data field collected, mark which serve the user vs which feed prediction products; the gap is the surplus 2. Design for user agency over data before regulators force you — exportability, deletion, opt-out from training 3. Watch the prediction imperative inside your team — every "we could collect X to improve Y" needs a counter-question: whose improvement?

⚠️ Pitfall: assuming "we don't sell data" exempts you from the critique. The relevant variable is what behaviours the system modifies, not whether the surplus is monetised externally.

Discussion

  1. Pick an AI feature in your stack. Map its extraction surface: service-data vs behavioural surplus. How big is the gap?
  2. If "Big Other" is already here, what is the most powerful refusal a single PM team can credibly make — and at what cost?
