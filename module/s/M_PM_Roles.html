PM Roles

This unit covers the six product-side job titles that get used interchangeably, the two axes that actually separate them, and what research on news product managers says about the job you are being trained for.

🎯 Learning objectives

# What you will be able to do The real problem it lets you answer
1 Distinguish the six product-side titles by what each one owns Job ads in Taiwan use these six titles almost interchangeably. If you cannot tell them apart, you cannot tell which ad you are qualified for.
2 Place any product job on two axes — inbound vs outbound, technical vs content — from its duties rather than its title "Two companies advertise Product Manager and the jobs are nothing alike." They are in different quadrants, and the title hides it.
3 Explain why the product role appears at the meeting point of editorial, business, and technology, and what that position costs the person holding it Why does a product team with a good roadmap still fail to get resources? Because bridging roles have influence without organisational power.
4 Judge which quadrant you are strongest in and what you would have to build to hold a conversation in the others This is the decision behind your first job application, and it is better made now than after the interview.

Six titles, one confusing job market

These are the technical-function roles you will rotate through in your team. They overlap in practice, and different companies draw the lines differently.

Title The one-line difference
Product Owner (PO) Owns the backlog and says what gets built next
Product Manager (PM) Owns the product's success — market, users, and requirements
Project Manager (PjM) Owns schedule, dependencies, and delivery, not the product's direction
Product Marketing Manager (PMM) Owns positioning, launch, and how the product is explained outward
Product Designer Owns how the product works as an experience
Interface Designer Owns how the product looks and behaves on screen

The title is unreliable; the duty sentence is not

Read past the title and find the sentence that says what the person would own. That one sentence places the job on both axes below, and it is more informative than the entire rest of the ad.

Inbound PMs face the product; outbound PMs face the market

The inbound side is where requirements are made: talking to users, writing the MRD and PRD, arguing about scope, sitting with engineers. The outbound side is where the product meets the world: positioning, launch, pricing narrative, the story that sales and marketing repeat. Both are called Product Manager. In a small company they are one exhausted person.

The technical–content axis decides which arguments you can win

A technical PM's authority comes from being able to argue about feasibility with engineers. A content PM's authority comes from being able to argue about audiences, editorial judgement, and what a reader will actually do. You are being trained for the second, in an environment where the first is becoming cheaper to attempt.

What the research says about this job in newsrooms

The product manager is a genuinely new role in journalism, and it has been studied. What the studies find is worth knowing before you apply for one.

The role sits where editorial, business, and technology meet

Kosterich (2021) finds that the core function of a news product manager is bridging three departments that have been separated for decades, each running on a different logic and a different set of priorities. A New York Times chief executive named giving digital product managers greater control as the single most important reason for the company's digital revenue passing 800 million US dollars in 2019. See product managers bridge editorial business technology silos in newsrooms and news product manager.

Bridging is not neutral: it is arbitrage

Kosterich and Royal (2024) theorise product managers as institutional arbitrageurs — they deliberately pull three incompatible logics together and work the differences between them to create value, which makes them a locus of change in the industry. Interviewees described themselves as "the link between everyone else". One repeated a headhunter's line: one department speaks duck, another speaks chicken, and somebody has to teach them both to speak goose. See + Product managers act as institutional arbitrageurs bridging editorial business and technology logics.

The most common tactic in the data was purpose difference — leveraging the fact that editorial, audience, business, and technology each want different things. Notably, this bridging is expected to happen inside existing journalistic values rather than overturning them, and that constraint is one reason product teams hold limited organisational power. See Purpose difference is the most common arbitrage tactic among news product managers.

Influence without power is the standing condition of the job

Resource scarcity forces product managers to cut features and raise funds continuously. One interviewee described the cycle: we need more funding, we need to fix the business model, we need more customers — and a good roadmap moves slowly anyway. A digital-native organisation chose not to launch comments, because nobody could moderate them. Product people are described as being in a precarious position, having to advise leadership on what not to do as much as what to do. See Resource scarcity forces product managers to limit features and continuously raise funds.

Who gets these jobs, and where

Product managers largely enter journalism from outside it, mostly from technology and business, and that outsider status is part of why they can push organisational change — the institutional-entrepreneurship prediction that peripheral actors open new arrangements. See product managers enter journalism from outside journalistic field. The role also grows fastest in digital-native organisations, whose structures resist a new role less than legacy newsrooms resist the vocabulary of "product" (product manager role grows fastest in digital-native news organizations). Kosterich's cross-national comparison finds the US role more professionalised than the UK's, tracking the difference between the two media systems (US news product managers more professionalized than UK counterparts).

For students · What that means for you specifically

You are entering from inside journalism, not outside it — the opposite of the typical route in this research. That cuts both ways. You will not need to prove you understand editorial value, which is the thing outsiders spend two years earning. You will have to prove the other two legs, which is what the technical weeks and the market weeks of this course are for.

⚠️ Common misconceptions

What students assume Why it is wrong What to do instead
"PM, PO and PjM are basically the same job" They differ on what they own: backlog, product success, and delivery Read the duties, not the title
"A content PM is a PM who is bad at technology" The two axes are independent. A content PM's authority comes from a different evidence base, not a weaker one Ask which argument the job needs you to win
"Product managers decide what gets built" In most organisations they have no authority and no reporting team. Influence comes from having the evidence and the document Notice how often "who decides" is somebody you have to persuade
"Bridging three departments means being liked by all three" Arbitrage means working the differences, which sometimes means making them visible. That is not the same as consensus Name the difference in purpose out loud, then propose which one wins this time
"If the roadmap is good the resources will come" The research says the opposite: good roadmaps stall on resource scarcity, and part of the job is advising on what not to do Bring a not-doing list to every resourcing conversation

Pair review

The situation. Two ads from the same media company land in your feed on the same day. Both say Product Manager. One reads: "work with engineers to define the requirements for our new app and decide what ships in each release." The other reads: "own the launch narrative for our subscription product and work with partners to grow the audience."

Start with these two, then swap in the three duty sentences you brought from the pre-class task.

  1. Place each job on the two axes, using only the duty sentence
  2. Find one ad where the title and the quadrant disagree
  3. Say which of their three you think they should apply for, and what they would have to learn for the other two

情境：同一家媒體公司的兩則職缺同一天出現在你的動態上，兩則的職稱都是 Product Manager。第一則寫：「與工程師合作定義新 app 的需求，決定每個版本要出什麼。」第二則寫：「負責訂閱產品的上市論述，與合作夥伴一起把受眾做大。」

先用這兩則開始，再換成你課前帶來的那三句職責句：

  1. 只看職責句，把每一份工作放進兩軸四格

  3. 說出對方那三則裡你認為他該投哪一則，另外兩則他還缺什麼
