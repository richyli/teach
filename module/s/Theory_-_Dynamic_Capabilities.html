Dynamic Capabilities

A short primer on Dynamic Capabilities: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

David J. Teece, Gary Pisano & Amy Shuen, 1997 — "Sense, seize, transform."

Core idea

  • Ordinary capabilities keep the lights on (operate, maintain, comply).
  • Dynamic capabilities reconfigure the firm to capture changing opportunities.
  • Three microfoundations (Teece 2007):
  • Sensing — scan markets, technologies, weak signals; spot opportunities and threats.
  • Seizing — commit resources, design business models, mobilise organisational capital.
  • Transforming — reconfigure assets and routines so the firm fits the new environment.
  • Business-model design is itself a seizing activity (Teece 2010): models are how value gets captured, not just delivered.

Lens questions for your project

  • What is our team's sensing routine — and is it pointed at the right horizon?
  • When we seize, do we commit real resources, or do we hedge and end up too small to matter?
  • What organisational routines block our transforming muscle? (Reorgs, budget cycles, KPI inertia.)
  • Are we confusing operational excellence (ordinary capabilities) with strategic adaptability (dynamic capabilities)?

Recommended reading

  • Teece, Pisano & Shuen (1997) "Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management," SMJ 18(7) — the founding paper. See Teece 1997 Dynamic.
  • Teece (2007) "Explicating Dynamic Capabilities," SMJ 28(13) — the sense / seize / transform tripartite. See Teece 2007 Explicating.
  • Teece (2010) "Business Models, Business Strategy and Innovation," Long Range Planning 43(2). See Teece 2010 Business.
  • Background note: Teece, David J.

Pair with Agentic AI

  • Move 1 — Use AI for sensing. Signal detection across markets, competitors, and weak signals scales beautifully — let AI read 100× more than your team can.
  • Move 2 — Keep humans in seizing. Committing organisational resources is a values-laden, accountable act; don't outsource it to a model.
  • Move 3 — Rebuild transforming routines when AI shifts what's feasible. The cost curve of every capability has moved; old reconfiguration playbooks may now be wrong.
  • Pitfall — Letting AI replace seizing decisions. AI surfaces options well; humans must choose, commit, and own the consequences.

Discussion

  • Map your last big strategic decision onto sense / seize / transform. Which step were you weakest on?
  • What are the two strongest signals AI could be reading for you right now that no human on the team currently has time to read?
  • If you had to transform one team routine in the next quarter to fit the AI shift, which routine — and what would replace it?
