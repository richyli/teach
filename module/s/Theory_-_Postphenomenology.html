Postphenomenology

A short primer on Postphenomenology / Technological Mediation: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Don Ihde (1990); Peter-Paul Verbeek (2005, 2011) — "Technologies don't just intermediate experience — they mediate and reshape what counts as real, good, or moral."

The core idea

Technologies are never neutral instruments. They co-shape the user: what is perceptible, what is doable, and what is morally available are all reconfigured by the artefacts we use.

Ihde's four human-tech relations: - Embodied — the tool becomes part of you (glasses, mouse) - Hermeneutic — you read the world through the tool (thermometer, dashboard) - Alterity — you relate to the tool as a quasi-other (chatbot, voice assistant) - Background — the tool shapes context without being foregrounded (HVAC, recommender)

Verbeek extends this: mediation is perceptual + practical + moral.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. Which of Ihde's four relations does this feature establish — and is that the relation we want? 2. What does this technology make easier to perceive? What does it push into the background? 3. What moral choices are pre-made by the design — for the user, by the artefact? 4. Where is the AI shifting from embodied tool to alterity quasi-other, and is the user prepared for that shift?

Recommended reading

  • Ihde 1990, Technology and the Lifeworld — the four relations originate here; see Ihde 2008 Postphenomenological
  • Verbeek 2005, What Things Do — moralising mediation, full statement
  • Verbeek 2011, Moralizing Technology — applied ethics via mediation
  • Verbeek 2020, "Politicizing Postphenomenology" Foundations of Science — see Verbeek 2020 Politicizing; also Verbeek 2023 Postphenomenology

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Mediation analysis on every AI workflow — what does Claude make easier to see? what does it render invisible? what moral choices does it pre-make? 2. Name the relation per feature — autocomplete is embodied, dashboards are hermeneutic, chat is alterity; mismatched relations break trust 3. Treat your Skill as moralising technology — you embed values whether you intend to or not; make them explicit in the system prompt

⚠️ Pitfall: the "AI as neutral tool" frame. Every Claude Skill mediates — the question is how, not whether.

Discussion

  1. Pick an AI-assisted workflow in your team. Run a mediation analysis: what is amplified, what is invisibilised, what moral choices are now made by the tool?
  2. If a Skill always moralises, who in your org should approve which morals get inscribed — and is that a PM, a designer, or a policy person?
