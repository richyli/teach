Pitch and Judge Skill Design

This unit covers how the two pitch days run, what a Judge Skill is, and why building one means writing your own values into something that will then grade your classmates.

W8 — Midterm Pitch

Each team presents the MRD finished in W7: 15 minutes plus 5 minutes of Q&A, scored by peers and the instructor against the midterm rubric. The Q&A is the exam. This consolidates W1 to W7, and the theory load is deliberately light so that the focus stays on defending the MRD.

W16 — Final Pitch and Judge Skill

A complete product proposal running market research → MRD → PRD → wireframe → risk → roadmap. On top of that, each team designs a Judge Skill: a Claude Code skill that scores other teams' pitches on the rubric dimensions — feasibility, theoretical grounding, AI craftsmanship, scope discipline.

Theory

Distributed Cognition (Hutchins) — judging is a cognitive system distributed across human peers, AI judges, and the rubric as a shared representation. Inscription Script Theory (Akrich) — designing a Judge Skill is literally inscribing your own values into an artefact that will go on to judge your classmates.

Hands-on — Judge Skill and final peer review

  1. Run your Judge Skill against every other team's pitch
  2. Combine Judge Skill scores, peer scores, and instructor scores into a single ranking
  3. Four awards follow: most feasible, most theoretically grounded, most creative use of Agentic AI, and best Judge Skill — the one whose scores agree most closely with the peer and instructor consensus

This closes the loop with the skill-building in W2 and W10.

How the session runs

The pitches. Fifteen minutes plus five of Q&A at the midterm; the full proposal at the final. The Q&A is live assessment, and while it happens the other teams are scoring with their own Judge Skills.

Running the Judge Skills. Each team feeds the other pitches into its own skill and exports the four dimension scores on the spot; Judge Skill scores, peer scores, and instructor scores are stacked into one ranking, which shows whose ruler tracked the consensus.

✍️ Take-home task

For the final: submit a working Judge Skill plus a reflection on what the AI judge caught that humans missed, and the reverse.

Cases

  • Claude Build Day Hackathon: the judges' three criteria — verifiability, real impact, deployability — map almost directly onto this module's four rubric dimensions.
  • Opus 4.7 Claude Code Hackathon: MaestrIA's "build the evaluation before the features" (nine dimensions against twelve real cases) is a student-discovered version of a Judge Skill.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Swap Judge Skill scoring results with another team.

  1. Compare the score their AI judge gave you with the weaknesses you already knew about
  2. Say whether the AI caught a scope gap you had missed
  3. Say whether it missed the theoretical grounding you were proudest of

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: what the AI judge caught that the human peers missed, and what the humans caught that the AI missed. This is the first draft of your final reflection.
