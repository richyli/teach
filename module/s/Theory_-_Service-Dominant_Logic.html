Service-Dominant Logic

A short primer on Service-Dominant Logic: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Stephen L. Vargo & Robert F. Lusch, 2004 — "Value is co-created with users — not delivered to them."

Core idea

  • Reject the goods-dominant view: a product is not a container of pre-made value.
  • Goods are simply vehicles for service. Service is the application of competences for the benefit of another.
  • Operant resources (skills, knowledge, relationships) outrank operand resources (matter, inventory).
  • Value is co-created at the moment of use, in a service ecosystem of interacting actors.
  • 11 foundational premises (FP1–FP11) build the worldview; FP10: value is always uniquely and phenomenologically determined by the beneficiary.

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Supporting theory in W14 Stakeholder Management — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.
  • It is never the week's Primary theory, so no report is assigned to it. It still earns marks when it does real work in a pitch.

The full week-by-week list is in Theory Report Schedule.

Lens questions for your project

  • Where exactly does value happen for our user — at purchase, at first use, at the 30th use?
  • What operant resources (skills, relationships, data, prior knowledge) does the user bring that we depend on?
  • Who else is in the service ecosystem — partners, complementors, regulators, the user's own social circle?
  • Are we co-creating value, or merely shipping it over the wall and hoping?

Recommended reading

  • Vargo & Lusch (2004) "Evolving to a New Dominant Logic for Marketing," Journal of Marketing 68(1) — the founding paper. See Vargo Lusch 2004 Evolving.
  • Vargo & Lusch (2008) "Service-Dominant Logic: Continuing the Evolution," JAMS 36(1) — the 10 (later 11) FPs in mature form.
  • Tregua, Brozovic & D'Auria (2021) Journal of Service Theory and Practice — 15-year systematic review. See Tregua Brozovic 2021 SDL Review.
  • Background notes: Vargo, Stephen L, Lusch, Robert F, service-dominant logic redefines exchange as service for service, service ecosystem is the dominant SDL trajectory in second decade.

Pair with Agentic AI

  • Move 1 — Design Agentic AI as a co-creator with the user, not a service-delivery channel that simply hands over output.
  • Move 2 — Audit your value-proposition canvas through the SDL lens: what operant resources does the user contribute? AI changes which of those become bottlenecks.
  • Move 3 — Map the service ecosystem explicitly when AI joins it as a new actor — what does it amplify, what does it crowd out?
  • Pitfall — Pricing as if value = output (tokens, API calls, deliverables) when value actually happens at the user's moment of use, often days later.

Discussion

  • Pick a feature you shipped last quarter. At what moment did value actually appear — and did anyone on the team notice?
  • Name three operant resources your user must bring for your product to work. What happens if they don't?
  • If your product became an Agentic AI co-actor rather than a tool, what would change in your pricing model and your support structure?
