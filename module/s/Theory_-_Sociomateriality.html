Sociomateriality

A short primer on Sociomateriality: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Wanda J. Orlikowski (2007); Paul M. Leonardi (2013) — "The social and the material are constitutively entangled."

The core idea

There is no purely "social" team and no purely "technical" tool — they only exist through each other in practice.

  • Reject the social-vs-technical split you inherited from MIS textbooks
  • AI artefacts have agency that emerges in practice, not in the spec sheet
  • The same Claude Skill is a different thing in two different teams

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Supporting theory in W3 Content Product, W9 Product Design & Wireframe, W13 Synthetic Data & AI Persona — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.
  • It is never the week's Primary theory, so no report is assigned to it. It still earns marks when it does real work in a pitch.

The full week-by-week list is in Theory Report Schedule.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. Where am I describing the tool and the team as if they were separable? 2. What practices produce this tool's agency in this team? 3. If I move the tool to another team, what materializes differently? 4. Whose body, screen, or hand is doing the work the slide deck attributes to "the system"?

Recommended reading

  • Orlikowski 2007, Organization Studies 28(9) — "Sociomaterial Practices"
  • Leonardi 2013, Information & Organization 23(2) — sociomateriality without the entanglement metaphysics
  • Mutch 2013, Journal of Information Technology — the critical reply you should read to keep the concept honest

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Practice trace — when the same Skill works in Team A and fails in Team B, document the practice, not the prompt 2. Material agency log — record where Claude refused, drifted, or surprised you; treat these as data, not noise 3. Reframe "AI literacy" — measure capability at the team-tool unit, not the individual

⚠️ Failure mode: using "sociomateriality" as a buzzword while still writing roadmaps that separate "tech work" from "change management."

Discussion

  1. Pick one AI tool you used this semester. What does it do in your team that it doesn't do in another?
  2. If we can't separate the social from the material, what happens to the PM concept of "requirements"?
