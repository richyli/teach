Activity Theory

A short primer on Activity Theory: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Doing the Theory Report on this? Start here, then read Engeström 1987 or 2001 itself — not this page. Looking for the taught unit? The full 30-minute teaching unit, with the product-planning application, the MRD/PRD mapping and the class activities, is Activity Theory.

Yrjö Engeström (1987, 2001) — "Subject + Tool + Object + Rules + Community + Division of Labor."

The core idea

Human activity is a 6-element system, and learning happens when contradictions between elements force the system to expand.

  • Subject acts on Object, mediated by Tools
  • Embedded in Community, governed by Rules and Division of Labor
  • 3rd-generation CHAT: two interacting activity systems, with a shared object

Contradictions are not bugs — they are the engine of expansive learning.

The activity triangle

            Tool
              |
   Subject — Object — Outcome
       \      |      /
       Rules  |  Division of Labor
              |
          Community

When AI joins as a new Tool, every other vertex shifts. Read the redesign through the triangle.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. Who is the Subject and what is the shared Object of this work? 2. When the new AI Tool enters, which contradiction surfaces first — Rules, DOL, Community, or Object? 3. Are we redesigning all six elements, or just dropping in a tool? 4. Where are the two activity systems whose objects I am trying to align?

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Diagnose with the triangle — when AI adoption stalls, locate the contradiction at a specific vertex 2. Redesign Rules and DOL alongside the tool, not after it ships 3. Two-system mapping — when your team's AI agent talks to another team's agent, draw both triangles and find the shared object

⚠️ Failure mode: treating AI as just a Tool upgrade. It almost always rewrites Rules and Division of Labor too.

Discussion

  1. For your final project, draw the activity triangle. Which vertex is most fragile?
  2. Where in your work this semester did a contradiction become a learning opportunity rather than a blocker?
