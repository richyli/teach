Actor-Network Theory

A short primer on Actor-Network Theory: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Bruno Latour, Michel Callon, John Law (1980s–2005) — "Follow the actors — including the non-human ones."

The core idea

Both humans and non-humans are actants. The social world is the temporary, fragile achievement of networks that hold them together.

  • Don't presume what counts as social, technical, or natural — trace what holds together
  • Networks form via translation (Callon's four moments)
  • Stability is an effect, not a starting condition

Callon's four moments of translation

  1. Problematisation — define the problem so others need you to solve it
  2. Interessement — lock potential allies into roles that fit your problem
  3. Enrolment — get them to actively play those roles
  4. Mobilisation — speak on their behalf as a unified network

Datasets, regulators, and language models can all be enrolled or refuse enrolment.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. Which non-humans (datasets, APIs, models, laws, devices) are doing political work here? 2. At which of Callon's four moments is my network failing? 3. Who is speaking for whom — and could that spokesperson be deposed? 4. What black box am I treating as one actor that is actually a fragile network?

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Non-human stakeholder map — list datasets, model providers, evaluation harnesses, regulators alongside human stakeholders 2. Translation diagnostic — when an AI rollout stalls, locate which of Callon's four moments collapsed 3. Black-box opening — every "AI integration" arrow on your slide is a network; trace one fully
