Sensemaking

A short primer on Sensemaking: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Karl E. Weick (1979, 1995) — "We make sense of the world by acting in it."

The core idea

Sensemaking is the ongoing retrospective process by which people give meaning to equivocal experience.

  • Enactment: you act first, the environment reacts
  • Selection: you pick a plausible story from the reactions
  • Retention: that story shapes the next round of action

The goal is not certainty — it's reducing equivocality just enough to keep moving.

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Primary theory in W4 Market Research, W15 Risk Management — a rotating pair opens those sessions with a 15-minute report on it.
  • Supporting theory in W7 User Research & MRD Assembly, W11 PRD / Product Requirement Document — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.

The full week-by-week list is in Theory Report Schedule.

Lens questions

When you face a product decision, ask: 1. What story am I telling about this market — and what action produced it? 2. What other plausible stories fit the same data? 3. Where am I treating equivocal signals as if they were unequivocal? 4. If this story is wrong, what will I see first?

Recommended reading

  • Weick 1995, Sensemaking in Organizations — the canonical seven properties of sensemaking
  • Weick, Sutcliffe & Obstfeld 2005, Organization Science 16(4) — sensemaking as the ongoing accomplishment of organizing
  • Maitlis & Christianson 2014, Academy of Management Annals — the field-defining review (see Maitlis Christianson 2014 Sensemaking Organizations)

(Weick scholar note: Weick, Karl)

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Alternative-narrative generator — ask Claude for three rival stories that fit the same evidence before you commit to one 2. AI pre-mortem — have the agent role-play a 6-month-post-launch failure and reverse-engineer the cause 3. Equivocality audit — feed your market brief to AI and ask "where am I claiming clarity I don't have?"

⚠️ Failure mode: using AI to manufacture confidence (premature closure) instead of holding equivocality open.

Discussion

  1. Recall a product decision where the team locked onto the first plausible story. What signals did you stop seeing?
  2. If sensemaking is retrospective, what does that mean for "data-driven" PM rhetoric?
