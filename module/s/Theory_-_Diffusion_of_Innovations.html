Diffusion of Innovations

A short primer on Diffusion of Innovations: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Everett M. Rogers (1962; 5th ed. 2003) — "Innovators → early adopters → early majority → late majority → laggards."

The core idea

Innovations spread through a social system over time, following an S-curve, via five adopter categories.

Five perceived attributes that drive (or block) adoption: - Relative advantage — better than what it replaces - Compatibility — fits existing values, needs, practices - Complexity — easy to understand and use - Trialability — can be tested with low commitment - Observability — results are visible to others

Plus: re-invention, opinion leaders, change agents.

Where this theory sits in this course

  • Supporting theory in W5 Competition Analysis — optional stretch material, and a useful contrasting lens in those weeks.
  • It is never the week's Primary theory, so no report is assigned to it. It still earns marks when it does real work in a pitch.

The full week-by-week list is in Theory Report Schedule.

Lens questions

When you face a launch decision, ask: 1. Which of the five attributes is our weakest? (Usually one dominates.) 2. Where on the S-curve are we honestly — and where do we claim to be? 3. Who are our opinion leaders, and have we resourced them? 4. What re-invention are users doing that we should formalise?

Recommended reading

  • Rogers diffusion of innovations — Rogers 2003 (5th ed.), the canonical text
  • Moore 2014, Crossing the Chasm (3rd ed.) — pragmatist gap, B2B tech focus
  • Greenhalgh 2004 Diffusion Innovations Service Organizations, Milbank Quarterly 82(4) — diffusion in complex service organisations

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. Curve mapping — ask an AI to scan competitor AI features (changelogs, X posts, reviews) and place each on the S-curve with a 12-month forward estimate 2. Chasm diagnosis — for AI-skeptical user segments, ask AI to enumerate what is blocking each of the five attributes 3. Opinion-leader detection — feed community data to AI to surface non-obvious early adopters

⚠️ Failure mode: generative AI compresses adoption phases unevenly across user segments. The classic S-curve assumes a single population; AI fractures populations into parallel curves moving at different speeds.

Discussion

  1. Pick an AI feature in your org. Where is it on the S-curve for power users vs. average users — and is the gap widening or closing?
  2. Rogers wrote in a one-curve world. Should we adopt parallel S-curves by user trust profile, or is that overfitting?
