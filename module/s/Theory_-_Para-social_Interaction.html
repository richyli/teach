Para-social Interaction

A short primer on Para-social Interaction Theory: what it claims, where it sits in this course, and the questions it lets you ask about your own product.

Donald Horton & R. Richard Wohl (1956) — "Audiences form one-sided relationships with media personas — and now with AI agents."

The core idea

Audiences experience media personas as if they were in a real social relationship — even though the persona cannot reciprocate.

Key distinctions: - PSI — single-encounter para-social interaction (during viewing) - PSR — durable para-social relationship (across encounters) - Illusion of intimacy — direct address, eye contact, name use - Reciprocity asymmetry — persona never actually responds to you - Transportation — narrative absorption that lowers critical distance

Originally about TV hosts. Now alarmingly relevant to AI agents.

Lens questions

When you face an AI-persona decision, ask: 1. Are we designing for PSI (single session) or PSR (durable bond)? 2. Where are we manufacturing illusions of reciprocity that the system cannot honour? 3. What happens at the breakdown moment — when the user notices the asymmetry? 4. Is our research method itself producing PSI artefacts (e.g., synthetic users that are too agreeable)?

Recommended reading

  • Horton & Wohl 1956, Psychiatry 19(3), "Mass Communication and Para-social Interaction" — the founding paper
  • Rubin & McHugh 1987, Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media 31(3) — PSR development and attachment
  • Hartmann & Goldhoorn 2011, Journal of Communication 61(6) — experimental tests of PSI mechanisms

(See also: Lou 2022 Social Media Influencers Trans-Parasocial for influencer-era extension)

Pair with Agentic AI

Three concrete moves for AI-assisted PM work: 1. PSI-aware persona design — design AI agents assuming users will para-social with them, and engineer healthy asymmetry cues in 2. Synthetic personas with disclosure — use AI personas in research, but always flag the PSI risk and triangulate with real users 3. Breakdown rehearsal — script the moment the illusion breaks (model swap, downtime, refusal) and design recovery flows

⚠️ Failure mode: synthetic users are too easy to relate to → researchers over-trust their feedback. The W13 lab compares PSI signals between synthetic and real users to expose this gap.

Discussion

  1. If a user prefers an AI agent's empathy over a human friend's, is that a feature or a harm? Who decides?
  2. Horton & Wohl wrote about TV hosts who could not see the audience. AI agents can (sort of) see the user. Does that make PSI obsolete, or far more dangerous?
