Content Products and Affordance Teardown

This unit covers the genres a content product can take, the difference between a feature and an affordance, and how an affordance connects to a business model that has to pay for itself.

Core

  • Content-product genres: news, podcast, newsletter, interactive, social.
  • Affordance versus feature; the Value Proposition Canvas; AI-native business models and unit economics.
  • Primary theory: Affordance Theory. Supporting: Sociomateriality, Surveillance Capitalism (Zuboff, on the political economy underneath "co-production").

Three layers of affordance

Perceived — what you can see at a glance: the buttons and entry points on the interface. Hidden — what the product does not surface: the things you only find by poking around, or by being an old hand. Actionable — what actually gets someone to do something: what this design is really telling the user to go and do.

Map the three layers separately. Only then can you see which behaviour the product is steering people towards.

A feature is what the product has; an affordance is what a user thinks they can do with it

A feature is a line on a spec sheet. An affordance is a relation between the artefact and a particular user in a particular situation. The same feature can afford completely different things to different audiences — and that gap is exactly where a redesign gets its leverage.

Connecting affordances to the business model

Take the affordance to the Value Proposition Canvas: which job does it get done, which pain does it relieve, which gain does it create? Then take it to AI-native unit economics — the token cost of each generation against what someone will pay. That is how you find out whether an affordance is worth having.

Hands-on — Affordance Teardown

Pick one existing AI content product: Perplexity, Granola, NotebookLM, or Substack.

  1. Map its perceived, hidden, and actionable affordances as three separate layers
  2. Redesign it for a non-obvious audience — a 70-year-old reader, for example
  3. Say which layer you had to change to make the redesign work

✍️ Take-home task

Submit your affordance map plus the redesign proposal.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Pair review

Swap products and maps with another team.

  1. Ask about one function they marked as hidden: why does the product not put it in plain sight — is it hidden for technical reasons, commercial reasons, or because it would frighten a beginner?
  2. Pick out the boldest single move in their redesign for the non-obvious audience
  3. Say whether that move changed a perceived affordance or an actionable one

Individual reflection

Write one sentence to keep: "I only realised today that a content product I use daily is quietly steering me towards __, and the choice it never showed me is ____."
