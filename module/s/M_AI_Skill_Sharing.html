AI Skill Sharing

This unit covers how to present one small thing you built in three minutes, how to judge whether someone else's tool transfers to your own situation, and why a share with no pitfall in it is an advertisement.

🎯 Learning objectives

This is a sharing and peer-critique unit. The goal is to turn one person's demo into something everyone can take away.

# What you will be able to do The real problem it lets you answer
1 Explain one small thing you built in three parts: what problem it solves, how it works with a demo, and one pitfall Why is "I built a complete system" worth nothing, while "this used to annoy me and now a small trick saves me half an hour" is worth a lot?
2 Take apart someone else's tool with three quick questions — would I use it, where could it save more effort, is there a better tool What would I have to change to use this in my own work? Connecting it back to your own situation is what makes it more than a compliment.
3 Judge whether you could reproduce a shared tool successfully, and report back A share with no pitfall is an advertisement; a share with a pitfall is teaching material. You can take the prompt, but do you know which line to change in a different situation? If not, you copied without learning.

What this slot asks of you

Two or three people each week, three minutes each: what AI tool you used this week, what real problem it solved, and which pitfall you hit.

Three requirements:

  • It has to be something you actually used on real work, not something you saw recommended
  • You must name a pitfall. A share with no pitfall is an advertisement
  • Paste the prompt or the steps into the shared document afterwards, so people can take it

Listeners have a job too: decide whether the tool still holds up in your situation. Taking the prompt is not the same as learning it. Knowing which line to change when the situation changes is.

How the slot runs

The share — problem → how you did it → one pitfall you hit, with a 30-second live demo. Quick critique — the room responds with the same three questions: would I use it, where could it save more effort, is there a better tool. Archiving — the presenter drops the artefact into the shared class toolbox with a one-line description of what it is for.

By the end of term this accumulates into a personal and a class-wide AI toolbox.

💬 Discussion and reflection

Individual reflection

Presenters, write one line this week: "What change did someone suggest that I had not thought of?"

Listeners, pick one tool someone shared and actually try it. Report back next time on whether you could reproduce it.
